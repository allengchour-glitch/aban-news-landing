What AI text-to-speech can do in 2026 — and what it can't

Quality has made a leap. For factual, short text, AI voices are barely distinguishable from human ones. But in long, emotional passages you can still hear the AI: the emphasis stays uniform, real vocal drama is missing. In practice that means:

✅ Worth it for

  • Explainer and product videos
  • Voiceover for social clips
  • Menu/phone prompts
  • Quick drafts before you book a real voice actor
  • Multilingual versions of the same script

⚠️ Not yet ideal for

  • Audiobooks with an emotional lead role
  • Ads that live off vocal character
  • Anything where a real personality is doing the selling
  • Voice clones without consent (legally tricky)

Frequently asked questions

Do AI voices sound real in 2026?
For short, factual text, yes — many no longer notice the difference. In longer, emotional passages you can still hear the AI. For explainer videos and drafts it's plenty good; for the audiobook lead role, not yet.
Can I use AI voices commercially?
With most providers, yes — the paid plan includes a licence, but read it case by case. And: never clone anyone without consent. Recreating a real voice without permission is legally tricky and ethically questionable.
What about data protection?
Most large providers (including Murf) host in the US. For marketing scripts with no personal data, usually unproblematic. As soon as personal content is being voiced: check the server location and data-processing agreement, or pick an EU provider.
