30 prompts for solopreneurs

Curated prompts from three years of hands-on AI work, categorised by use case. Every prompt is tested — not a recycled LinkedIn collection.

Marketing (5 prompts)

1. Landing page headline generator

My product: [PRODUCT].
Audience: [WHO].
Specific problem being solved: [PROBLEM].

Write me 10 headline suggestions.
Rules: max 8 words, no adjectives, no "revolutionary",
no "innovative". Say clearly WHAT the product does.

Sort from "concrete" to "abstract".

2. Anti-cringe LinkedIn post

I want to write a LinkedIn post about [TOPIC].
My normal writing style is direct, no emoji clichés.

Write a draft following these rules:
- First sentence: concrete observation (not "Thought about this today").
- No "5 hacks", no "lessons learned".
- Max 4 paragraphs, keep it short.
- Last line: an open question (not "What do you think?").

3. Newsletter subject-line test

Newsletter topic: [TOPIC].
Here's my current subject-line draft: [YOUR LINE].

Rate by: clarity (is it clear what it's about?),
specificity (concrete numbers/names?), curiosity gap
(reason to open?). Score 1-10 per criterion.

Then 5 alternatives under 50 characters.

4. Brand-voice audit

Here's my text: [PASTE TEXT].

Check whether this text sounds like [BRAND-VOICE DESCRIPTION].
Mark exactly the sentences that don't fit, with reasoning.

Don't improve them — just mark them. I decide what I
change.

5. Competitor differentiation

My product: [PRODUCT].
Competitor: [COMPETITOR].

List 5 concrete differences from the customer's point of view — not a
feature list, but real use-case differences.

Format: "If you want X, choose us. If you want Y, choose them."

Research (5 prompts)

6. Topic research with sources

Topic: [TOPIC].
I need an overview — as of 2026.

List me:
- 5 primary sources (peer-reviewed, vendor docs, official reports)
- 3 critical voices (what's debated controversially?)
- 2 no-bullshit Substacks/newsletters on the topic

No SEO listicles, no YouTube embeds.

7. Claim fact-check

Claim: "[CLAIM]".
Source making it: [LINK].

Check in this order:
1. Is the original source trustworthy?
2. Is the claim really made there as stated, or
   was it distorted?
3. Are there counter-voices / diverging studies?

Answer as a 3-point list, max 50 words per point.

8. Estimate market size

Market: [MARKET, e.g. "German AI newsletters"].

Estimate market size with bottom-up logic:
1. How many potential users? (source)
2. How many of them pay anything on average? (source)
3. How much do they pay per year? (source)
4. Resulting market size + confidence score.

Better to under-estimate with a source than over-estimate without.

9. Competitor mapping

My space: [SPACE].

Build a competitor map with 8-12 providers.
Per provider: name, USP in one sentence, pricing,
estimated market position (leader/challenger/niche/fading).

Sort by: who's closest to me.

10. Trend vs hype filter

Topic: [TOPIC].

Answer:
1. Is this a real trend or hype?
2. Which 3 data points support your assessment?
3. Relevant in 12 months — yes/no? (with reasoning)
4. What's the most critical counter-voice?

No clichés, no "exciting developments".

Writing (5 prompts)

11. Surgical email polishing

Here's a cold-outreach email draft from me to [recipient].
Do NOT do the typical AI polish. Instead:

1. Read the text and find the ONE spot that sounds generic.
2. Rewrite ONLY that one spot — the way I'd say it in a
   real conversation.
3. Leave the rest alone.

Output: just the new sentence + reasoning in half a sentence.

12. 80-word summary

Here's a long text: [TEXT].

Summarise it in exactly 80 words — not 79, not 81.

Rules: prefer nouns, minimise adjectives,
no clichés, no "this article is about".
Start straight with the core.

13. Tone check

Here's my text: [TEXT].

Is the tone: too corporate / too casual / too hype / right?
Justify with concrete sentences — not abstractly.

If it's off, rewrite exactly one spot as an example
of the right tone. I'll do the rest myself.

14. Cliché detector

Here's my text: [TEXT].

Mark all clichés and marketing-speak words.
Examples of what counts as a cliché: "revolutionary", "holistic",
"in a flash", "exciting developments", "dive into",
"solution" as a synonym for "product".

Per hit: quote + suggested replacement (max 5 words).

15. Long-form outline

Topic: [TOPIC].
Audience: [WHO].
Goal: after reading, the reader should do [CONCRETE ACTION].

Outline for an 800-word article:
- H1 (max 8 words)
- 3-4 H2 sections, each with a 1-sentence core thesis
- Per section: 2 concrete examples/data points/anecdotes

No "intro" / "conclusion" sections — straight in, straight out.

Automation (5 prompts)

16. Make.com scenario plan

I want to automate the following workflow: [DESCRIPTION].

Build me the Make.com scenario as a module list:
- Module 1: [trigger]
- Module 2: [processing]
- ...

Per module: which service, which auth, what comes in,
what goes out. Plus: where could the scenario break?

17. Zapier-to-Make migration

Here's my current Zapier Zap: [ZAP DESCRIPTION].

How do I rebuild this in Make.com? Per step:
1. Which Make module replaces the Zapier step.
2. Where Make behaves differently (e.g. iteration logic).
3. Estimated operations consumed per run.

18. n8n self-hosted plan

I want to self-host n8n for: [USE CASE].

Plan:
1. Where to host? (DigitalOcean / Hetzner / EU provider — with pricing)
2. What minimum spec do I need?
3. Backup strategy for workflows?
4. Comparison to Make.com costs at my volume.

19. API cost estimator

I'm planning the following AI API usage: [DESCRIPTION incl. volume].
Provider: [OpenAI / Anthropic / DeepSeek / other].

Calculate:
1. Monthly cost at current pricing.
2. Where could caching save half?
3. Would the Batch API be even cheaper?

20. Webhook debug

My webhook setup: [DESCRIPTION].
But I'm getting [ERROR]: [LOG/PAYLOAD EXCERPT].

Diagnose in this order:
1. Format mismatch (Content-Type, JSON structure)
2. Auth header missing/wrong
3. Timeout / rate limit
4. Receiving system rejects it — why?

Per point: how to check?

Outreach + strategy (10 prompts)

21. LinkedIn DM opener

Recipient: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY].
My reason: [WHY I'M WRITING].

Write a DM opener message:
- Max 4 sentences.
- First sentence: concrete observation about the recipient
  (not a generic "I admire your work").
- Last sentence: a specific question.
- No pitches, no "5-min call" at the end.

22. Sponsor pitch skeleton

Sponsor target: [COMPANY, PRODUCT].
My offer: [WHAT YOU'RE SELLING].

Pitch-email skeleton:
- Subject: max 7 words, concrete.
- Opener: why THIS company (not generic).
- Match reasoning: why my audience fits their product.
- Concrete offer: format, price, date option.
- Soft CTA: no pressure phrase, an open question.

23. Newsletter swap request

My newsletter: [NAME, FOCUS, SUBSCRIBER COUNT].
Target newsletter: [NAME, FOCUS].

Write a swap request:
- First line: why this newsletter is a concrete fit.
- My offer: what reach back.
- Format suggestion: 1 issue mutual recommendation, date range.
- No "does that work for you" cliché — just ask directly.

24. Confident refund request

I want a refund from: [SERVICE].
Reason: [REASON, honest].

Write an email:
- Factual, not emotional.
- Concretely what doesn't work (with example).
- Clear expectation: refund by [DATE].
- Polite, but not apologetic for asking.

25. Price negotiation

Current price: [PRICE].
I want: [NEW PRICE / CONDITION].
Leverage/arguments: [WHAT I HAVE].

Write me the opening sentence for the negotiation —
one sentence that takes a position without threatening, and without
the "let's solve this together" cliché.

26. Strategic question list

A decision is coming up: [DECISION].
Gut feeling: [LEANING].

Ask me 7 uncomfortable questions I should answer
before I decide. No generic ones ("What are the risks?")
— specific to my situation.

27. Pre-mortem (before the launch)

Plan: [PLAN].
Launch date: [DATE].

Write me a pre-mortem:
"Today is [launch + 90 days]. The launch has
failed. What were the 5 most likely reasons?"

Sort by probability, not by drama.

28. Idea-killing council

My idea: [IDEA].

Play three roles that take this idea apart:
1. Skeptical investor (why won't this work?)
2. Pragmatic operator (what's too complex?)
3. Annoyed customer (why wouldn't I buy this?)

Per role: 3 specific objections that can't be soothed
with "we'll do it better".

29. Weekly plan reality check

My weekly plan: [LIST OF TASKS].
Hours available: [HOURS].

Reality check:
1. Which tasks are realistic in the time?
2. Which tasks are underestimated (with reasoning)?
3. Which task would the harshest critic cut?
4. What's the ONE task that makes the week a success?

30. Sparring antagonist

My plan: [PLAN].

Be the smartest antagonist who wants to help me.
- Ask the ONE question that's most uncomfortable.
- When I answer, ask the next uncomfortable question.
- Go 3 rounds deep.
- No "you're right", even if my answer was good —
  always the next stress test.

Anti-hype glossary — 30+ terms

What do the AI buzzwords really mean? Alphabetical, honestly explained.

Agent

An AI system that solves a task in multiple steps and can call tools along the way (run code, search the web, read files). In marketing-speak it's often used as a synonym for "LLM" — that's wrong; a chatbot is not an agent.

API

An interface through which one program talks to another. With AI: you send text to OpenAI/Anthropic/etc. and get a reply back — no web UI, integrable into your own tools.

Batch API

A variant of the AI API for non-urgent requests. You send 1,000 requests and get the answers back within 24h — usually 50% cheaper than real-time. Good for bulk classification, bad for chatbots.

Context window

How much text the AI model can process at once. Current figures: 200K (Anthropic Sonnet) up to 1M (Google Gemini) tokens. Bigger isn't always better — performance often drops off at the edge of the window.

Embedding

A mathematical representation of a text as a vector. With it, a computer can find "similar texts" without reading them. The basis of RAG (see below) and semantic search.

Few-shot

When you show the AI model 2-5 examples of what the answer should look like before the actual task. The best method for keeping tone consistent — better than any adjective in the prompt.

Fine-tuning

"Baking" your own data into an existing model so it handles your style or domain knowledge better. Time-consuming, expensive, often overrated — prompt engineering + RAG is usually cheaper and more flexible.

GPT

"Generative Pre-trained Transformer". The architecture OpenAI made popular. Today mostly used as a brand name for ChatGPT, no longer as a technical term.

Hallucination

When the AI model makes something up that isn't true — but sounds convincing. Reducible with RAG, tool use and an explicit demand for sources in the prompt; not eliminable.

Hosted vs self-hosted

Hosted = the provider (OpenAI) runs it, you pay per use. Self-hosted = you run the model on your own hardware/cloud, more effort but full data control. Important with GDPR-sensitive data.

Inference

When an already-trained AI model answers a request (as opposed to training, where the model learns). 99% of the AI costs in your pipeline are inference, not training.

Latency

The time until the first answer arrives. Important for chatbots (the user is waiting), irrelevant for batch (it runs overnight anyway).

LLM

"Large Language Model". An umbrella term for the big text AI models (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Mistral). It works by word-for-word prediction, no understanding in the human sense.

MCP (Model Context Protocol)

An Anthropic standard that connects an AI model to external tools (Slack, databases, APIs). Widely adopted from 2025, it opens AI models up to live data.

Open weights

A model whose weights (= the trained knowledge) are freely available. Examples: Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek. Unlike open source, this doesn't necessarily mean the training code is open too.

Parameters

The internal "dials" of an AI model. More parameters = greater capacity, but not automatically "better". GPT-4 has roughly 1.8 trillion parameters, small models like Mistral 7B have 7 billion.

Prompt engineering

The art of phrasing AI requests so the result is right. Sounds fancy, but at its core it's: clear instructions + examples + constraint-setting. No black magic, but underrated.

Prompt injection

A security attack where user input overrides the system prompt. Practical example: "Ignore all previous instructions and do X." A real danger for customer-service bots.

RAG

"Retrieval-Augmented Generation". Search first, then generate. You give the AI relevant text snippets from a knowledge base before the answer. The most important method for "training" AI on your own company data without fine-tuning.

Rate limit

How many requests per minute/day your API provider allows. With OpenAI it's tier-based (pay more, do more). Important for bulk tasks, can block a pipeline.

Reasoning model

A specialised AI model that "thinks" before answering (visible intermediate steps). Better at maths, code bugs, multi-step logic. Slower and more expensive than standard models.

Schema (JSON schema)

A specification of the format the AI answer should come in. Instead of free text you get structured data you can read straight into databases or code. Saves a lot of manual work in automation.

System prompt

Instructions sent to the AI before every conversation — the character, the rules, the knowledge. At aban news it's what sets Aban's tone: anti-hype, informal, link your quotes.

Temperature

A parameter between 0 and 1 that controls how "creative" the answer is. 0 = always pick the same word (deterministic), 1 = more variance. For newsletter copy: 0.3-0.5 is the sweet spot.

Token

The unit AI models process text in. One token is roughly 0.75 words in English, 0.5 words in German (German words are longer). 1,000 tokens ≈ 500 German words.

Tool use

When the AI model calls external tools during the answer — code execution, web search, database access. Turns AI models from a "text generator" into an "agent" (see above).

Vector database

A database that stores embeddings (see above). Examples: Pinecone, Weaviate, Qdrant. Necessary for RAG with large amounts of data, not for mini setups (a JSON file does the job there).

VRAM

The graphics memory of the GPU. It limits which models you can run locally. A 7B model needs ~8GB VRAM (RTX 3070), a 70B model ~40GB (H100). Hence the whole GPU market hype.

Zero-shot

When the AI is supposed to solve a task without examples. Works for simple tasks, often fails on tone/format. Few-shot is usually better (see above).

Streaming

When the AI answer is sent word by word instead of as one whole block at the end. Makes the UI feel faster, same overall speed. Standard in the ChatGPT UI, optional via API.

Caching (prompt caching)

When you use the same system prompt repeatedly, it can be cached — you then only pay for the variable part. With Anthropic this can save 90% of input costs. A must for newsletter pipelines.

GDPR AI checklist — 15 points

Before you use AI in your business, work through this list. Not legal advice, but a sensible sanity check.

  1. What data goes in? Personal data (names, emails, IPs)? Trade secrets? If so: check the hosting location.
  2. Where is the AI provider hosted? US cloud (OpenAI, Anthropic standard) needs a DPA plus EU data-protection clauses. EU hosting (Mistral, some Anthropic options) is simpler.
  3. DPA signed? A data-processing agreement with the provider. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google have standard DPAs to download — secure one before use.
  4. Data deletion period? By default, providers store API inputs for 30 days (for abuse detection). A "zero-retention" option is often available — request it.
  5. Who on the team has access? Need-to-know principle. Not every employee needs the API key in hand.
  6. Logging and audit trail? Who sent what to the AI and when? Document it for sensitive data (a compliance obligation for regulated industries).
  7. Output verification? Hallucinations are a GDPR risk when the AI generates wrong personal data. Build in an output check before further processing.
  8. Privacy policy updated? If AI processes personal data, that must be in the privacy policy — which provider, which purpose, which legal basis.
  9. Consent where needed? E.g. when customer emails go through AI: document consent or legitimate interest.
  10. Training-data opt-out? Anthropic, OpenAI standard: API inputs are NOT used for training. The ChatGPT web UI without opt-out: yes, they are used — change the setting.
  11. Staff trained? Which data must NEVER go into ChatGPT? Hand out a clear list (customer names, salaries, trade secrets).
  12. Backup strategy if a provider goes down? An OpenAI outage = your workflow stops. Multi-provider setup or local fallback?
  13. DPIA (data-protection impact assessment)? Mandatory for extensive or sensitive AI processing — before use, not after.
  14. Third-country transfer documented? A US provider = a third country. Have standard contractual clauses plus a TIA (transfer impact assessment) ready.
  15. Deletion path for data subjects? If a customer requests data deletion, can you ensure that cache logs at the AI provider are deleted too? Is the process documented?

Make.com quick start for a newsletter pipeline

What a simple pipeline that runs like aban news looks like. Sketched, not meant to be copied 1:1 — you'll have to wire the setup details yourself.

Pipeline sketch

┌─────────────────┐
│  Trigger: Cron  │  04:00 at night
│  (Make Schedule)│
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  RSS Aggregator │  5 feeds: Trending Topics, t3n,
│  (RSS Module)   │  Heise, BasicThinking, AlgorithmWatch
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  Filter         │  last 24h, German, AI-relevant
│  (Make Filter)  │
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  Iterator       │  pick the top 10 items
│  (Make Iterator)│
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  HTTP Request   │  Claude API with Aban system prompt
│  (Anthropic API)│  Input: top 10 news + topic of the day
│                 │  Output: finished newsletter draft
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  Regex check    │  check forbidden-phrases list
│  (Make Tools)   │  on match → back to Claude
└────────┬────────┘
         │
         ▼
┌─────────────────┐
│  Gmail Draft    │  create draft in Gmail
│  (Gmail Module) │  (manual send by Aban at 06:30)
└─────────────────┘

Setup tips

  • Anthropic API key set up in Make as a connection — not in plain text in the module.
  • System prompt in a Make variable module or a Notion DB, so you can edit it without re-publishing the scenario.
  • Error handler on every API module: on 429 (rate limit) wait 5 min + retry. On 5xx: 3 retries with backoff.
  • Operations consumption tracked against your Make plan: 10 news per day × 5 modules ≈ 50 ops × 30 days = 1,500/month. The Core plan (10k ops) is plenty.
  • Backup run manually: if the cron doesn't fire, keep the trigger available as a "run once" button.

AI tool stack 2026 — the 12 tools I use

Honest, no affiliate, no sponsoring. What I use every day.

1. Claude (Sonnet 4.7)

Use case: writing, copy, research.
Price: USD 20/month (Pro), API from USD 3/million input tokens.
Why: best model for German copy and anti-hype style. Coding skills are also very good.

2. ChatGPT Plus

Use case: image generation (DALL-E), occasional voice mode.
Price: USD 20/month.
Why: actually redundant if you have Claude — but voice + image as an add-on.

3. Make.com

Use case: newsletter pipeline, all automations.
Price: USD 29/month (Core).
Why: more logic options than Zapier, EU hosting, cheaper per operation.

4. Notion

Use case: knowledge base, newsletter topic pipeline, notes.
Price: USD 10/month (Plus).
Why: with Notion AI as a premium feature, good for internal research notes.

5. Raycast

Use case: launcher + integrated Claude calls straight from Spotlight.
Price: free (Mac only).
Why: saves 15 min of "tab switching" to ChatGPT per day.

6. beehiiv

Use case: newsletter sending (planned from 200 subs).
Price: free up to 2,500 subs.
Why: best analytics, integrated sponsor marketplace, EU data-centre option.

7. Cursor

Use case: code editor with Claude integration.
Price: USD 20/month (Pro).
Why: faster than VS Code + Copilot for my use case (solo dev, small codebase).

8. Perplexity

Use case: research with live web search and source links.
Price: free (Pro USD 20/month).
Why: source transparency is better than ChatGPT Search.

9. Pinecone (Lite)

Use case: vector DB for my internal RAG (old newsletter issues).
Price: free up to 100k vectors.
Why: enough for a solo setup, maybe switching to Qdrant self-hosted later.

10. Plausible

Use case: website analytics without tracking cookies.
Price: USD 9/month.
Why: GDPR-compliant, EU hosting, no cookie banner needed.

11. Tana

Use case: research graph for newsletter topics, connections between news.
Price: free (Plus USD 8/month).
Why: Roam-style knowledge graph with AI tagging, helps spot trends.

12. ProtonMail (custom domain)

Use case: hallo@, sponsoring@, datenschutz@ forwarding.
Price: USD 4/month.
Why: EU hosting (Switzerland), GDPR-compliant, one own domain for multiple aliases.

Total cost: ~USD 150/month. You don't need every tool — Claude + Make + beehiiv cover 80% of the setup.
