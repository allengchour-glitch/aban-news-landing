In depth: the anti-hype filter in 60 seconds

Every week something is "the end of" or "the revolution for". Most of it is neither. You don't need to be an expert to separate substance from marketing — you just need five questions and a minute.

Five questions for any AI headline

  • Who benefits from the claim? Someone raising a round or selling a product talks about "breakthroughs" differently than someone who has to work with them.
  • Demo or production? A curated demo shows the best case. What's interesting is how something holds up on the third Tuesday with real, ugly data.
  • Cherry-picked? An impressive example is one example, not proof. Ask for the average, not the highlight.
  • Reproducible? Can others reproduce the result independently — or is there just the one viral screenshot?
  • What does it really cost? Including setup, learning curve, rework and your time. "Saves 10 hours" is worthless if onboarding costs 40.

Investor deck vs. Tuesday morning

The biggest gap in the AI discourse runs between what announcements promise and what actually works on a normal workday. Keep that gap in mind and you make better decisions — and save yourself expensive mis-purchases others only regret months later.

Reading benchmarks without fooling yourself

Numbers look objective but are often optimised for the test. Always ask: who measured, against what, under which conditions — and does that match your use case? A top score in a lab test says little about your inbox.

Scepticism isn't pessimism. It's about saving your attention (and money) for what truly proves itself. That's the idea behind aban news.
