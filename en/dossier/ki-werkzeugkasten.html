In depth: how to judge AI tools honestly

More AI tools launch than any human could ever test — and new ones arrive daily. So the goal isn't to know them all, but to find the two or three that make your work measurably better. Everything else is well-made distraction. That's what the toolbox is for: not "look what's new", but "is this worth it for you".

Five questions before any subscription

  • Does it solve a real problem of mine? Not "is it impressive", but "does this task annoy me often enough that a fix pays off".
  • Does it save more time than it costs? Setup, learning and the daily back-and-forth all count. A tool that saves five minutes but needs ten to manage is a net loss.
  • Where does my data live? Hosting location, data processing, what happens to your inputs. In the DACH region that's not a detail — it's often the deciding question.
  • How big is the lock-in? What happens if the provider shuts down, doubles the price, or drops the feature you depend on? The easier it is to leave, the safer the subscription.
  • Can a tool I already have do this "well enough"? The most honest saving is the subscription you never start.

The honest 14-day test

Never test on the glossy demo, but on a real, boring project from your everyday work. Run it for two weeks in parallel with your current method and note two things: where did it actually save time? And where did you have to redo the work because the output wasn't good enough? After 14 days that note decides — not the gut feeling right after the first "wow".

The costliest mistake: the subscription graveyard

Most people don't pay too little for tools — they pay too much for tools they don't use. A tool you touch every day beats ten you "tried once". Clean up once a quarter: what did you actually open in the last four weeks? The rest goes.

Rule of thumb: workflow first, tool second. Start with the tool and look for the workflow afterwards, and you've built yourself work you didn't have before.

Frequently asked questions

Which AI tools are actually worth it?

The few that solve a real, recurring problem of yours and save more time than they cost. Rule of thumb: a tool you use daily beats ten you tried once. Test for 14 days on real work, not on the demo.

How do I test an AI tool seriously?

In parallel with your current method, on a real project, for two weeks. Note where it saved time and where you had to redo the work. Then the note decides, not the first wow.

What should I watch for with tools in the DACH region?

Where the data is processed (EU or third country), whether there's a data-processing agreement, and how big the lock-in is. More in the data-protection dossier.
