What AI video can do in 2026 — and what it can't

The pace is breathtaking, the hype even faster. Short clips often look stunningly good today. But "finished video at the push of a button" is marketing, not everyday work: you still need a concept, you cut, you fix hands, text and logic. AI takes steps off your hands — it doesn't replace the craft. In practical terms:

✅ Worth it for

  • Short B-roll and mood clips (text→video)
  • Explainer and training videos with an avatar & voice
  • Captions, translation and social editing from existing footage
  • Multilingual versions of the same video
  • Quick drafts before you produce something expensive

⚠️ Not yet ideal for

  • Long films with a coherent storyline
  • Exact branded products, logos and text in the frame
  • Consistent characters across many scenes
  • Real people as avatars without their consent

The EU catch: privacy & rights

This niche is heavily US-centric. Most tools do not host in the EU — for footage with no personal data that's usually uncritical, but as soon as real people, voices or customer data come into play, check the server location and the data-processing agreement. Two points that tend to get lost in the hype:

  • Non-EU providers: CapCut (ByteDance) and Kling (Kuaishou) are Chinese providers. Be especially cautious with sensitive content.
  • Consent & rights: never recreate anyone as an avatar or voice without their permission. And: check the licence for commercial use per tool before you publish anything.

We deliberately rank EU-leaning providers higher in the radar (e.g. Submagic from France, Synthesia and VEED from the UK) — clearly labelled, without pretending there's already a complete European all-in-one solution.

Frequently asked questions

Can I create a finished video at the push of a button?
For short clips (5–10 seconds), yes, with impressive results. But a multi-minute film with a clear thread doesn't appear at the push of a button — you cut and assemble scenes yourself. AI takes work off your hands, but it replaces neither the concept nor the edit.
Which tool fits which purpose?
Text→video (Runway, Pika, Luma, Kling, Sora, Veo) for short clips; AI avatars (Synthesia, HeyGen) for explainer videos with a talking presenter; repurposing (Descript, OpusClip, Submagic) for social clips and captions from existing footage. You'll find the comparison in the AI Video Radar.
What about privacy and rights?
Heavily US-centric — most tools don't host in the EU. With no personal data it's usually uncritical; for real people, voices or customer data, check the server location and data-processing agreement. CapCut (ByteDance) and Kling (Kuaishou) are non-EU providers. And: never recreate anyone without their consent.
Do I need my own voice for AI video?
You'll often combine image and voice from two tools: the video from a text→video or avatar tool, the voice from a text-to-speech tool. What AI voices can do in 2026 is on our AI voices page.
