Kagi vs Perplexity: what matters when choosing

If you are facing the choice between Kagi and Perplexity, it largely depends on what you are really looking for. If you want a privacy-friendly, ad-free search engine that gives you precise and customisable results, then Kagi is the better choice. It is ideal for users who value privacy, are willing to pay for it, and prefer a search experience they can shape themselves — essentially a tailored Google alternative without the usual marketing mechanisms.

Perplexity, on the other hand, is your tool if you want fast, summarising answers to complex questions delivered directly with source references. It is less a classic search engine and more an AI assistant that prepares information for you. So if you research often and want to save yourself the tedious clicking through search results in order to get an understandable answer right away, then Perplexity is the more suitable choice for you.

FAQ

What is the fundamental difference between Kagi and Perplexity?

Kagi is a search engine that gives you results without ads and tracking and lets you influence the ranking of sources. Perplexity is more of an "answer engine" that understands your question and gives you a summarising answer with the most important sources directly, instead of just a list of links.

Which tool is better if I care about data protection?

Kagi places a very high value on data protection and promises not to store your search queries or use them for advertising. Perplexity is an AI tool that accesses various sources to answer your questions, but it too aims to handle data responsibly.

Do I have to pay for these tools?

Yes, both Kagi and Perplexity are paid services. They usually offer different subscription tiers that differ in the scope of usage or the available features. In return you get an ad-free and often more capable experience than with free alternatives.

Is one of the tools easier to use for beginners?

Perplexity is often more intuitive for beginners, because you simply ask a question and get a direct answer. Kagi also offers a simple search bar, but the many customisation options can feel a bit overwhelming at first, though they are quickly learned.
