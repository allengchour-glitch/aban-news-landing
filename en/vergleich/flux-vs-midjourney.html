Flux vs Midjourney: what matters when choosing

You're facing the choice between Flux and Midjourney for creating images with artificial intelligence. To make the right decision, first consider exactly what you have in mind: Do you want quick and straightforward, appealing images for everyday use, for example for social media or presentations? Or are you looking more for a tool that gives you maximum creative freedom and control over every detail, even if it requires a bit more learning? Your answer to this question is the first and most important step.

If ease of use and fast results matter to you and you don't want to dive deep into technical details, a solution like Flux might be a better fit. If, on the other hand, you're looking for a tool that lets you realise very specific and unique visual ideas, and you don't shy away from a certain learning curve, then Midjourney is probably the better pick for you. Also bear in mind that some tools offer direct integration into other programs, which can simplify your workflow.

FAQ

What's the main difference between Flux and Midjourney if I've never worked with AI images before?

Think of Flux as a clever assistant that gives you lots of templates and easy ways to get good images quickly. Midjourney, on the other hand, is more like a blank canvas and a box full of colours, with which you can design everything from scratch yourself — if you know how.

Which tool is better if I only occasionally need a few images for my website or social media?

For occasional, quick images that should look good, a tool like Flux is often more practical. It's usually easier to use and delivers appealing results without much effort, which is plenty for everyday needs.

I want to create very specific and artistic images. Which tool gives me more control?

If you're after maximum creative control and want to realise very specific, artistic visions, Midjourney is usually the better pick. It offers more settings to fine-tune every detail of your images, but it also requires more learning.

Do I need to know a lot about "prompts" (the text inputs that describe the image) with either tool?

With both tools you enter text descriptions. With Flux the prompts are often simpler and there's more guidance. With Midjourney you can achieve significantly better results through more precise and detailed prompts, but that takes some practice.
