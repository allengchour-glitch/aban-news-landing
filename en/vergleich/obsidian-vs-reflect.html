Obsidian vs Reflect: what matters when choosing

When you're choosing between Obsidian and Reflect, it ultimately comes down to how you prefer to organise your thoughts and notes and what your priorities are. If you want a flexible, customisable solution that runs locally on your computer and gives you full control over your data, then Obsidian is often the better choice. It takes a little setup and initiative, but rewards you with a tailored environment that you can shape precisely to your needs — ideal for anyone who enjoys "tinkering" and values data protection.

Reflect, on the other hand, is the right choice if you want a simple, ready-to-use solution that automatically syncs your notes to the cloud and takes the thinking off your hands. It's ideal if you want to get started quickly, have no appetite for configuration and prefer a lean interface. If ease of use and automatic availability across all your devices matter more to you than absolute control over your data or the ability to customise everything down to the last detail, you'll be happier with Reflect.

FAQ

What is the biggest difference in data storage?

Obsidian stores your notes as plain text files locally on your device, which gives you full control. Reflect stores your notes encrypted in the cloud, which makes access from anywhere easier, but means your data sits with an external provider.

Which tool is easier to learn for beginners?

Reflect is usually easier to learn, as it offers a clearer structure and fewer customisation options. Obsidian has a steeper learning curve, as there are many features and extensions that you first have to understand and set up.

Which tool is better if I have many different kinds of notes (e.g. journal, project planning, ideas)?

Both can do it, but in different ways. Thanks to its plugins and free-form structure, Obsidian is extremely flexible for all kinds of notes. Reflect is also well suited thanks to its simplicity and linking, but less customisable in terms of display and organisation.
