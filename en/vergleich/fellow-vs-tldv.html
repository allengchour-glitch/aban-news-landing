Fellow vs tl;dv: what matters when choosing

If you're looking for a tool that helps you plan and structure your meetings better, then Fellow is a good choice. It is particularly strong when it comes to creating agendas, collecting notes and distributing tasks – everything that matters before and during the meeting. Fellow is ideal for teams that place a lot of value on clear preparation and want to make sure everyone is on the same page before they even start talking. It helps improve communication and ensures that everyone really knows what to do, and that this remains traceable even after the meeting. The learning curve is moderate, and there are good integrations with other business tools.

If, on the other hand, you're looking for a tool that focuses more on what happens *after* the meeting, then tl;dv is the better choice. It records your meetings, transcribes them (i.e. turns speech into text) and automatically summarises the key points. That's super handy if you can't attend every meeting or want to review later what was discussed without listening to the whole recording. tl;dv is especially useful for teams that have many online meetings and need to extract the core information quickly to make decisions or share content. It helps you save time by delivering the essence of a conversation.

FAQ

Which tool is better if I have many external partners in meetings?

tl;dv is often more practical here, as it makes recording and summarising easier, which you can then simply share with external participants. Fellow is more geared towards structuring internal meetings.

I'm unsure about data protection. Which is safer for the DACH region?

Both tools are headquartered outside the DACH region. It is important to check each provider's data protection policies carefully and, if necessary, request a data processing agreement to ensure compliance with local regulations such as the GDPR.

My team isn't very tech-savvy. Which tool is easier to use?

tl;dv is often more intuitive for the core function of recording and summarising. Fellow requires a little more onboarding to make the most of all the meeting planning and task management features.
