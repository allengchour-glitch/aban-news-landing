DeepL Write vs LanguageTool: what matters when choosing

If you write texts in German and care about style and expression, DeepL Write is often the better choice. It helps you make your sentences not only grammatically correct but also more fluent and varied. DeepL Write is especially useful when you frequently write texts for a broader audience where the right tone matters, such as marketing copy or blog articles. It is very intuitive to use: you simply paste in your text and receive rewording suggestions, which makes getting started very easy. For data protection, it's worth knowing that DeepL is a German company and is therefore subject to the GDPR.

LanguageTool, on the other hand, is ideal when your main concern is the pure correction of spelling, grammar and basic punctuation. It's a reliable helper for emails, reports or other texts where being error-free matters, but stylistic finesse matters less. It often offers more integration options directly into your writing programs, which can make your workflow easier. If you're on a tight budget or looking for a free solution, LanguageTool's basic version is often sufficient and a good option. LanguageTool also has its origins in Germany, which is a plus on data protection for many in the DACH region.

FAQ

Which tool is better when I write texts for work where professionalism matters?

For professional texts where style and expression matter, DeepL Write is often the better choice, because it goes beyond pure error correction and helps you improve your phrasing. LanguageTool is good for basic error-free writing, but less so for stylistic nuances.

I write a lot in German, but also occasionally in English. Which tool handles both well?

DeepL Write has the edge here, since DeepL is generally known for its excellent translation and language-improvement features across multiple languages. LanguageTool also supports many languages, but DeepL Write is often ahead when it comes to stylistic improvement suggestions.

I'm unsure about data protection. Which tool is the safer choice here?

Both tools originate in the DACH region (Germany), which means they are subject to strict European data protection rules (GDPR). For most users, both are a good choice in terms of data protection, since they have to comply with these rules.

I use various programs to write (Word, browser, email client). Does one of the tools integrate better?

LanguageTool often offers a broader range of direct integrations as a browser extension, add-on for office programs, or in email clients. DeepL Write works primarily through its own website or as a desktop application, which means you usually have to copy your texts there.
