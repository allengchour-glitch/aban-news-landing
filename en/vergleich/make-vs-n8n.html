Make.com vs n8n: what matters when choosing

You are facing the choice between Make.com and n8n to automate recurring tasks. Put simply: if you are looking for a fast, visual solution that works right away and where you do not have to worry about the technology behind the scenes, then Make.com is often the better choice for you. It is like a construction kit where you snap ready-made building blocks together, and it works well for marketing, sales or simple office workflows — if data protection in the DACH region matters to you and you are willing to pay for it.

If, on the other hand, you are looking for maximum control over your data, want to run the tool on your own server and perhaps even have a small team that is comfortable with technology, then n8n is well worth considering. It is more flexible, but also more demanding to set up and maintain. For developers, agencies or companies with strict data protection policies that want to save costs and keep full control, n8n offers the freedom you need, even if getting started takes a bit more time and technical understanding.

FAQ

What is the biggest difference in the pricing model?

Make.com is a subscription model where you pay for usage, similar to Netflix. n8n is available as a free open-source version that you host yourself, or as a paid cloud version that then resembles Make.com but gives you more flexibility.

Which tool is easier to learn for someone with no prior knowledge?

Make.com is usually easier to learn. It has a very intuitive graphical interface where you click workflows together visually. n8n requires a bit more onboarding and an understanding of technical concepts, especially if you set it up yourself.

Which tool is better if data protection in Germany, Austria or Switzerland (DACH) is especially important to me?

If maximum control over your data is important to you, n8n offers the advantage that you can run it on your own server in the DACH region. With Make.com your data sits on their servers, and even though these are GDPR-compliant, you have less direct control.

Are there differences in the number of programs I can connect?

Both tools offer a very large number of integrations, i.e. connections to other programs. For most common applications such as Google Sheets, Slack or CRM systems you will find a suitable interface with either. There is rarely a decisive difference here in everyday use.
