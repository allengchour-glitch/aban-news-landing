Consensus vs Elicit: what matters when choosing

When you're choosing between Consensus and Elicit, a simple question helps: is your main goal to find answers from many scientific studies quickly and easily, without having to dig in deeply? Then Consensus is often the better choice. It's like a search engine that delivers the most relevant findings on your question directly — ideal if you just want a quick overview or evidence for a particular claim. Watch the costs here, since the free features are often limited, and bear in mind that integration with other tools is fairly rudimentary.

If, on the other hand, you're looking for a tool that supports you more comprehensively in analysing and synthesising research, maybe even for a systematic literature review, then Elicit is probably the better fit. It helps you not only find studies but also structure and compare them, offering more depth for more complex projects. Getting started takes a little more effort, but in return you get a more powerful tool that helps you truly understand and process research findings. Bear in mind that data protection, especially in the DACH region, is an important point with both tools, since they often run on servers outside Europe.

FAQ

Which tool is easier to use if I've never worked with AI tools before?

Consensus is generally easier for beginners. It works much like a normal search engine: you type in your question and get results right away. Elicit takes a bit more learning, since it offers more complex features for analysis.

I need to systematically work through many studies on a topic. Which tool helps me more with that?

For a systematic review and analysis of many studies, Elicit is the stronger choice. It offers features to organise, compare and filter out key information from studies, which is very useful for more complex research projects.

Are there big differences in cost, or can I use both for free?

Both tools often offer a free version or trial, but with limited features. For the full feature set you'll need a paid subscription with both. Compare the exact offers here, since prices and included services can differ.

What about data protection if I'm processing sensitive research data?

You should be careful with data protection on both tools, especially when processing sensitive data. Many AI tools, these included, often have their servers in the USA, which can be problematic under data protection law in Germany, Austria and Switzerland (DACH). Always check the privacy policies and consider whether your data is suitable for this kind of processing.
