Replicate vs Together AI: what matters when choosing

You're facing the choice between Replicate and Together AI for using AI models. In short: if you want to try out ready-made AI applications quickly and easily, or build them into your existing software without worrying about the technology behind them, Replicate is often the simpler choice. It's like an app store for AI models, where you find ready-made solutions and can use them straight away — which is especially handy if you don't have a large development team or only need AI features occasionally.

Together AI, on the other hand, is more for you if you want more control over the AI models — for example if you want to adapt ("fine-tune") a particular model to your own data, or generate very large volumes of text or images. It gives you the raw compute power and the tools to run and customise models yourself. That does require a bit more technical understanding, but for special requirements or very high usage volumes it can be more cost-effective and flexible.

FAQ

I just want to quickly generate an image. Which tool is easier?

For quickly generating images, Replicate is usually easier. You find ready-made models there, type in your prompt and get a result straight away. It's like an online tool that's ready to use right away.

I have my own data and want to train an AI model on it. Which tool helps me better with that?

If you want to adapt an AI model with your own data, Together AI offers better options for that. It provides the infrastructure to train and customise models, which is usually not the main focus at Replicate to that depth.

I'm not a programmer. Can I still use one of the tools?

Replicate is often more user-friendly for non-programmers, as many applications can be used directly via a simple interface. Together AI usually requires a bit more technical understanding or the help of a developer to use the models optimally.

What if I'm worried about data protection, especially in the DACH region?

Both providers are US companies, which means they are subject to US data protection laws. For sensitive data in the DACH region you should check carefully with both providers how they handle data and whether that is compatible with your requirements. A blanket recommendation is difficult here.
