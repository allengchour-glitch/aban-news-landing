Dust vs Glean: what matters when choosing

If you are facing the choice between Dust and Glean, a simple consideration helps: is your goal to quickly build your own AI applications that are tailored precisely to your processes, or are you looking more for an intelligent search engine for your company? Dust is for those who want to roll up their sleeves and create custom AI tools — for example for texts or data analysis. It is like a construction kit that lets you develop your own „little AI helpers“ even without programming knowledge, which you then integrate into your daily workflows. Do bring a certain willingness to get to grips with the logic of building tools, even though it works without code.

Glean, on the other hand, is the better choice if your main problem is finding and using information in your company quickly. Think of it like a super-intelligent Google search that scans all your internal documents, chats and programs and delivers exactly the answers you need. The focus here is less on building your own AI features and more on the efficient use and connection of existing data sources. For companies in the DACH region that place great value on data protection and want easy integration into existing systems, both solutions offer different approaches that you should examine closely.

FAQ

Who is Dust better suited to?

Dust is ideal for teams or individuals who want to build their own AI applications without having to code. If, for example, you want to create a template (a "prompt") for emails or reports into which you simply drop your text, this is the right place. It takes a bit of learning to get the most out of the building blocks.

Who is Glean better suited to?

Glean is the right choice if you are looking for a central search engine for all your company data. It connects your tools like Slack, Confluence or Google Drive and helps you quickly find answers or share knowledge. The focus is on finding and organising information, not on building your own AI applications.

What is the biggest difference in usage?

The biggest difference lies in the approach: with Dust you create new AI features for specific tasks. With Glean you use AI to better find and connect existing information in your company. Dust is more of a "creator tool", Glean a "finder tool".

Are there differences in data protection for the DACH region?

Both tools are US-based, which always requires a careful review of server locations and data protection policies when sensitive data is involved. It is advisable to ask the providers directly about specific compliance certifications and the option of data processing within the EU, to make sure they meet DACH requirements.
