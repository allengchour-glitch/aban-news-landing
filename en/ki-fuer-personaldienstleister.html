AI for staffing agencies — where it really saves time

Temp work and recruitment live on people, trust and fast contact. Alongside that runs a mountain of writing: job ads, emails to candidates and client companies, internal documents, posts. That is exactly where AI helps — in the office and in communication, not in deciding about people. Here is what makes concrete sense and where hard limits apply.

What this is not about

AI does not make hiring decisions. It does not select anyone, does not sort anyone out and does not award points to candidates. Anyone who lets people be rated automatically risks discrimination under equal-treatment law — and moves into the high-risk zone with hard obligations under the EU AI Act. Just as little do CVs, names or personnel data belong in an AI tool; that is a question of data protection, not of technology. What remains is the office: texts, emails, documents, communication. And there is plenty of that in the staffing business.

1. Draft job ads — appealing and free of discrimination

A good job ad has to convince in seconds, name the tasks clearly and stay linguistically clean — no age limits, no gender-specific phrasing, the mandatory "(m/f/d)". Give the AI the plain key facts of the role (the work, the requirements, the location, what you offer) and have a lively, readable draft built from that. You can specifically ask it to watch for non-discriminatory language. The fine-tuning and the legal check you do yourself — AI just gets you faster from a blank page to a usable text.

2. Communication with candidates and client companies

Acknowledgements of receipt, proposed appointments, follow-up questions, status updates, rejections — and in parallel the coordination with the client companies. Every one of these messages should sound friendly and appreciative, the rejection especially. Have templates built that are respectfully worded and put no one off, then adapt them case by case. Important: do not enter real names or candidate data into the tool. You work with general building blocks and add the personal parts only at the end in your email program.

3. Structure internal texts, checklists and onboarding documents

An induction checklist for new staff in the dispatch team, a health-and-safety briefing for external workers before their first assignment, a structured onboarding process, a handover list for holiday cover — you need such internal documents rarely and they cost unnecessary time to write. Enter your bullet points, and AI turns them into a cleanly structured, complete document. You decide what goes in on content; it handles the ordering and the wording.

4. Draft social media and employer-branding posts

You find good people today via LinkedIn, Instagram or your own careers page too — and that demands regular posts: an employee portrait, a note about an open role, a piece on why it is worth working through your company. AI gives you several variants in different tones within minutes, from which you choose the right one and sharpen it. That way your employer branding sounds lively without you writing every post from scratch. It only becomes authentic through your concrete examples — and only you know those.

5. General info texts and FAQs for the website

How does temp work actually work? What does the service cost candidates? How is a placed worker insured? What is the first step for a client company? You write such info texts and FAQ answers rarely and start over each time. AI gets you quickly to an understandable draft in plain language. You have to be careful with anything legal — insurance, the supply of temporary workers, contract questions: what AI writes about it, you check against the applicable law and your own terms before it goes online.

Honest limits:
  • No automated candidate selection or AI scoring of people. That carries discrimination risks under equal-treatment law, and recruitment counts as a high-risk application under the EU AI Act. The hiring decision is always made by a human.
  • Do not enter candidate or personnel data into AI tools — no CVs, no names, no contact details. That is personal data under the GDPR. The chatbot should only see general, anonymous text.
  • AI sometimes invents facts and unintentionally phrases things in a discriminatory or legally imprecise way. Check every text before it goes out — job ads, contracts and rejections especially.
  • AI is an office tool, not a legal adviser. Questions about the supply of temporary workers, equal-treatment law, contracts or insurance you do not outsource to a chatbot.

Which tools fit?

To start, a single chatbot is enough — ChatGPT or Claude — fed only with general, anonymous text. Anyone who wants to be on the safe side picks tools with EU hosting and takes a quick look at the data protection terms, precisely because the personnel field involves an unusual amount of sensitive data. Frequently asked questions

May AI select or rate candidates?
No. Automated pre-selection or AI scoring of people is delicate: it carries discrimination risks under equal-treatment law, and recruitment counts as a high-risk application under the EU AI Act with strict obligations. The hiring decision is always made by a human. AI helps with writing and organising, not with sorting people out.
May I enter CVs or candidate data into AI tools?
No. CVs, names, contact details and information about specific people are personal data under the GDPR and do not belong in an AI tool. The chatbot should only ever see general, anonymous text — for example a task description with no link to a real person.
Where does AI save the most time in a staffing agency office?
With writing and structuring: drafting job ads, creating appreciative emails to candidates and client companies, structuring internal checklists and onboarding documents, drafting social media and employer-branding posts and writing general info texts or FAQs for the website.
Do I still have to check AI texts myself?
Yes, always. AI sometimes invents facts and unintentionally phrases statements that come across as discriminatory or are not legally clean. Read every text before it goes out — job ads especially for non-discriminatory language, and contracts or rejections for accuracy.
