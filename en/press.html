150-word bio (long-form, e.g. conference speaker page)

Aban (Allen Chour) is a Swiss solopreneur and the author of aban news — the first daily German-language AI newsletter for self-employed people, marketers and AI-savvy pros across DACH. Mon to Fri mornings, 3-5 minute read, with three to five news items per issue, a tool of the day and a prompt you can copy right away.

Aban has used AI tools daily for three years for writing, research and automation. What annoyed him about German AI newsletters: too weekly, too hype, too much press-release recycling. So he builds the thing he wants to read himself — direct, personal, with his own opinion visible.

Off the page, Aban lives in Switzerland, runs a Shopify setup on the side and answers every newsletter reply himself. Topics he's especially interested in: GDPR-compliant AI workflows for DACH companies, open-weights models with EU hosting, and how solopreneurs build viable businesses out of newsletters.

Pre-approved quotes

These statements can be attributed to Aban directly. If you need something specific — email Aban, and you'll usually get a new quote within one working day.

“95 percent of DACH AI newsletters are auto-translations of English sources. You notice it by the third sentence — the context of what's actually relevant here is missing.”

— Aban, on the market gap in German-speaking Europe

“Anti-hype isn't a style choice, it's a survival strategy for German solopreneurs. If you read 'This AI changes everything!' emails every morning, you learn nothing and grow numb.”

— Aban, on the tone of aban news

“When a tool is described as 'fundamentally new', it's usually fundamentally old. Real innovation rarely needs an adjective barrage.”

— Aban, on rating tools

“GDPR-compliant AI workflows aren't a compliance obstacle, they're a sales argument. DACH companies don't buy the slickest US vibe, they buy the thing that doesn't blow up in the audit at 2pm on a Thursday.”

— Aban, on AI adoption in German SMEs

“A newsletter without an opinion of its own is an RSS feed with an ego problem. If I can't say what I think of a tool, I haven't tested it long enough.”

— Aban, on the editorial line
