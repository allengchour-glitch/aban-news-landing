How the calculation works

1. Your target revenue

Target salary + business costs + profit buffer = what your business has to bring in per year. Many people count only the salary and forget costs and reserves — and then work below their worth.

2. Your real working days

365 days minus weekends, holidays, public holidays and sick days. What's left are your actual working days — usually far fewer than you'd think.

3. Only billable hours count

Acquisition, quotes, bookkeeping, training: not every working hour ends up on an invoice. Realistically that's 50–70 %. This is exactly where most people miscalculate.

4. The fair hourly rate

Target revenue divided by the hours you can really bill = your minimum hourly rate. Anything below that means paying out of pocket. This calculator does the maths in real time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the calculated hourly rate gross or net?
It's your net hourly rate without VAT. On the invoice — unless you are a VAT-exempt small business — VAT is added on top (which you pass on to the tax office). You cover income tax and social contributions out of your target revenue yourself — so factor them into your target salary.
Why is my hourly rate higher than an employee's hourly wage?
Because as a self-employed person you cover everything yourself: no paid holiday or sick days, no employer social contributions, your own insurance, your own equipment, slow periods and unpaid acquisition. A seemingly "high" hourly rate often just covers your costs.
How many billable hours are realistic?
At 40 hours a week you theoretically work ~1,700 hrs/year. Of those, usually only 50–70 % are billable. Anyone who assumes full utilisation with 100 % billable hours almost always sets their hourly rate too low.
Are my inputs saved?
No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser (JavaScript). No data is sent to a server, nothing is stored, no tracking. You can even use the page offline once it has loaded.
Does this also apply to Austria and Switzerland?
Yes. The calculation logic is the same everywhere; the region setting selects the currency (€ or CHF) and the approximate number of working days. Tax and social security systems differ — factor those into your target salary individually.
